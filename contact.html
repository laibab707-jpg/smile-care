Dental emergency

Emergency guidance

This is general information, not medical advice or a diagnosis. In a life-threatening emergency, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

Get urgent medical help straight away if

  • Swelling is spreading across your face or neck
  • You have trouble breathing or swallowing
  • Swelling comes with a fever and you feel very unwell
  • Bleeding does not stop after about 10 minutes of firm pressure
  • You had a serious injury to your head, face or jaw

See a dentist as soon as possible for

  • Severe or getting-worse toothache
  • A tooth that has been knocked out
  • A broken or cracked tooth with pain
  • A lost filling or crown with pain or a sharp edge
  • A painful, swollen gum or abscess-like bump

General first-aid tips while you arrange care

  • Knocked-out adult tooth: hold it by the crown (the white part), not the root. If it is dirty, rinse it gently without scrubbing. Keep it moist (for example in milk) and get to a dentist urgently.
  • Toothache: rinse your mouth with warm water and clean gently between teeth. For pain relief, follow the instructions on the product label or ask a pharmacist.
  • Bleeding after injury: press firmly with a clean cloth or gauze.
  • Broken tooth: rinse your mouth and keep any pieces. Do not chew on that side.

This page provides general guidance only. For urgent care, contact a licensed local clinic or emergency service directly.
